\section{The Continuing Bayes Institute Case}

\subsection{3 types of sale, 1 institutional standard}

As Bayes Institute scales from its initial foundation toward an enduring academic institution, it commercializes 3 distinct capability offerings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) Technical Studios:} Advanced, cohort-based capability studios for working software engineers and data scientists;
\item \textbf{Curricular College Partnerships:} Credit-bearing elective courses and laboratory programs integrated into university engineering curricula;
\item \textbf{Enterprise Capability Services:} Targeted diagnostic assessments and workflow engineering programs for corporate development teams.
\end{enumerate}
Although the procurement mechanisms, pricing tiers, and decision cycles across these 3 markets diverge substantially, they must share an unyielding institutional standard:
\begin{itemize}
\item Identify an authentic, verified operational problem before proposing an intervention;
\item Establish technical and logistical fit through structured diagnostic benchmarking;
\item Demonstrate competence via transparent, empirical proof artifacts;
\item Make all financial, workload, and legal obligations legible in written agreements;
\item Strictly reject commercial transactions that require promises the institute cannot honor.
\end{itemize}
A founder must never assume that commercial traction in 1 market validates another. An enthusiastic response from an individual working engineer does not prove that a university provost possesses budget. Separate pipeline records, conversion tracking, and unit contribution accounting are non-negotiable.

\subsection{D2C sales should begin with diagnosis and self-selection}

For an applied artificial intelligence studio at Bayes Institute, customer acquisition must reject high-pressure marketing tactics, manipulated scarcity countdowns, and emotional career panic. Instead, the admissions journey is engineered as a transparent diagnostic process:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Transparent Course Specification:} The program landing page explicitly articulates target learner profiles, mandatory mathematical and programming prerequisites, exact weekly workload demands (12 to 15 hours), code review turnaround standards, tuition fees, and explicit non-goals;
\item \textbf{Substantive Diagnostic Exercise:} Prospective applicants complete an optional 45-minute technical diagnostic evaluating Python code comprehension, vector operations, and error debugging;
\item \textbf{Structured Fit Consultation:} Applicants who pass the diagnostic schedule a 20-minute admissions consultation with a technical instructor.
\end{enumerate}
The admissions interview explores the applicant's current professional engineering responsibilities, past learning bottlenecks, and available calendar bandwidth. If the applicant lacks foundational prerequisites or cannot commit the required study hours, the instructor explicitly recommends foundational textbooks or open-source preparation rather than taking their tuition fee.

\subsection{A hypothetical learner conversation that does not begin with the pitch}

The following dialogue demonstrates the diagnostic sales methodology in practice:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Founder:} ``Tell me about the last production AI problem you were directly responsible for debugging.''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Engineer:} ``We launched a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline for customer support, but our evaluation framework keeps failing. We see unpredictable hallucinations, and our senior staff lacks confidence in our test suites.''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Founder:} ``What interventions have you attempted internally? What were the results?''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Engineer:} ``We watched several online video tutorials and experimented with open-source prompt evaluation packages, but nobody on our team knows how to mathematically benchmark retrieval precision under adversarial inputs.''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Founder:} ``Here is our evaluation rubric and a sample anonymized code review from our recent studio. Take 2 minutes to inspect it. Does this level of mathematical and code scrutiny align with what you need to solve this problem?''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Engineer:} ``Yes, exactly. This shows the error analysis we are missing.''\\[0.5ex]
\textbf{Founder:} ``Our studio requires approximately 12 to 15 hours of intense, hands-on code development every week for 8 weeks. We evaluate independent assignments, and our certificates verify assessed technical competence: we do not offer university degrees or guaranteed job placements. Based on your current work schedule, can you protect that calendar time?''
\end{quote}
If the engineer confirms schedule availability, the founder extends a formal enrollment invitation. If the engineer hesitates, the founder advises postponing enrollment until their professional schedule stabilizes.

\subsection{College sales: the 1st meeting must discover governance}

When an engineering college dean expresses interest in integrating Bayes Institute's AI curriculum into their undergraduate program, an inexperienced founder immediately delivers a lengthy presentation detailing syllabus modules. A seasoned consultative seller begins by mapping institutional governance:
\begin{itemize}
\item Which academic department owns the curricular requirement (Computer Science, Data Science, or Information Technology)?
\item Does this offering constitute a mandatory departmental core course or an open elective?
\item What formal committee approvals (Board of Studies, Academic Council, University Syndicate) are statutory prerequisites?
\item What exact student cohort will participate (e.g., 120 3rd-year engineering undergraduates)?
\item Which internal faculty members will serve as designated teaching assistants and lab coordinators?
\item What line-item budget has been authorized, and who possesses the statutory signing authority to execute the institutional memorandum of understanding?
\end{itemize}
Attempting to author an elaborate customized syllabus before discovering the university's governance process guarantees wasted administrative effort. The appropriate initial commercial deliverable is a bounded, paid pilot: specifying exact student numbers, lecture hours, laboratory rubrics, and milestone-based disbursement dates.

\subsection{Enterprise sales: connect capability to a real workflow}

When a corporate engineering executive requests ``generative AI upskilling for 50 developers,'' the founder must avoid prescribing a generic corporate lecture series. The sales motion must deconstruct the specific operational workflow that is failing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Reconstruct the exact engineering process where model development stalls;
\item Determine whether the failure stems from missing developer competence, corrupt training datasets, absent cloud compute infrastructure, or unsupportive executive leadership;
\item Test whether educational training is the appropriate organizational intervention.
\end{enumerate}
If the bottleneck is caused by absent cloud GPU infrastructure or restricted corporate data permissions, educational upskilling will achieve zero business impact.

A responsible enterprise commercial offer begins with a paid \textbf{Technical Competency and Workflow Diagnostic}:
\begin{itemize}
\item Assessing 20 developers across a standardized benchmarking task;
\item Analyzing sample failure logs under formal non-disclosure and data privacy agreements;
\item Delivering an executive baseline report identifying specific architectural and skill deficiencies.
\end{itemize}
The broader enterprise training contract is proposed only if the diagnostic demonstrates that human capability is the limiting constraint.

\subsection{The founder's availability changes what can be promised}

Because the founder intends to maintain full-time external employment, Bayes Institute cannot operate under the assumption of unconstrained executive availability. The commercial sales process must incorporate personal scheduling boundaries into every contract:
\begin{itemize}
\item Instructional lectures and student diagnostic calls must be scheduled strictly during evenings and weekends;
\item Bespoke weekday on-site enterprise travel or emergency daytime consulting must be explicitly excluded;
\item Total active teaching commitments across D2C cohorts and enterprise pilots must never exceed 15 hours per week.
\end{itemize}
Every prospective transaction must pass a formal \textbf{Capacity Gate} prior to proposal submission:
\begin{equation}
H_{\text{committed}} + H_{\text{new deal}} \le H_{\text{max}} \quad (15 \text{ hrs/week}).
\end{equation}
If a lucrative ₹10 lakh enterprise contract requires on-site delivery on Tuesday mornings, the founder must decline the engagement or subcontract delivery to a qualified adjunct faculty member. Protecting external employment obligations, personal health, and instructional excellence takes precedence over revenue maximization.
