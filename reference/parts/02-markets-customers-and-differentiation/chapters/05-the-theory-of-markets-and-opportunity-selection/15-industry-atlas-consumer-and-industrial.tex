\section{One Economic Logic, Diverse Operational Realities}

An entrepreneur commercializing an industrial flow valve, a packaged biscuit brand, enterprise security software, specialized management consulting, or an advanced technical institute does not confront an identical market-entry puzzle.

While the underlying laws of economics (bargaining power, unit contribution, substitutability, and cash conversion) are universal, their physical manifestations vary wildly across sectors. Quality observability, capital intensity, procurement friction, distribution bottlenecks, and regulatory oversight differ by orders of magnitude. \textbf{Mechanically transplanting a growth tactic from consumer internet to enterprise education is a recipe for rapid failure.}

\section{FMCG: Population Is Not Shelf Space}

An entrepreneur developing a packaged nutritional snack observes that 140 crore people eat food every day. This observation is mathematically true and commercially meaningless.

A viable opportunity demands a concrete consumption occasion (a breakfast commute, a mid-afternoon energy slump, an athletic recovery ritual) supported by truthful functional differentiation.

In physical retail, the market structure is brutally concentrated at the distribution layer. Large supermarket chains and regional distributors demand 30\% to 45\% trade margins, slotting fees for eye-level shelf placement, and promotional allowances. A food product must withstand transport vibrations, resist heat spoilage, maintain 90-day shelf stability, and generate sufficient inventory turnover to justify its retail footprint.

\textbf{Securing an initial bulk order from a wholesale distributor is merely sell-in; it is not proof of authentic consumer sell-through.} If shoppers do not pull the product off the shelf repeatedly at full price, distributors will return unsold inventory and terminate the account.

\section{Commodities and Industrial Manufacturing: Delivered Cost Rules}

In standardized industrial goods (fasteners, steel piping, bulk chemicals, basic electronic capacitors), buyers evaluate precise engineering specifications and delivered prices. In this domain, \textbf{inventing cosmetic brand differentiation is an expensive illusion.}

The founder's opportunity lies entirely within operational efficiency: superior raw material procurement, localized supply security, lower scrap rates, automated quality inspection, optimized working capital financing, or strategic geographic proximity that slashes logistics expenses.

A rigorous feasibility study models \textbf{through-cycle unit economics}: energy inputs, freight tariffs, raw material price shocks, scrap losses, equipment depreciation, compliance fees, and credit default risk. A brief macroeconomic commodity price boom can create an illusion of profitability that evaporates the moment global spot prices normalize.
