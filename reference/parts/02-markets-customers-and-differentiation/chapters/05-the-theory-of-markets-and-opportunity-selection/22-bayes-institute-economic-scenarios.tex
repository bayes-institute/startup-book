\section{The First Opportunity Is Not the Biggest Revenue Line}

Suppose Bayes Institute can successfully win a single ₹6,00,000 enterprise consulting engagement, but fulfilling the contract requires the founder to personally execute 150 hours of custom, non-standardized software scripting over 4 weeks under strict corporate confidentiality.

The fee is attractive. However, \textbf{the engagement generates zero reusable educational assets, builds zero public brand reputation, and threatens corporate employment obligations.} At the end of the project, the founder has cash in the bank, but is no closer to building an enduring educational institution.

Conversely, a smaller D2C studio generating ₹3,00,000 or a college lab generating ₹3,00,000 might yield slightly lower net cash, but produces a modular 8-week curriculum, automated coding evaluation engines, 10 enthusiastic student testimonials, and a repeatable instructional system.

\textbf{Compare candidate opportunities not by headline revenue alone, but by cash contribution, founder-hour consumption, operational risk, delivery repeatability, and long-term capability accumulation.}

Notice the vital operational realities revealed by this model:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=12pt,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item While Route C (Enterprise) generates the highest nominal residual (₹1,20,000), it demands 25 hours per week for 6 consecutive weeks, \textbf{violating the founder's 20-hour weekly sustainable bandwidth.}
  \item Route A (D2C) collects tuition upfront before classes commence, providing immediate working capital to fund teaching assistants and software tools.
  \item Route B (College Pilot) requires upfront delivery while freezing cash collection for 90 days in college accounting bureaucracy, requiring the founder to fund operational expenses out of pocket.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What Empirical Evidence Would Break the Deadlock?}

The optimal strategic route is not chosen by ideological preference; it is chosen by \textbf{which counterparty provides definitive behavioral commitment first}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=12pt,itemsep=2pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{For Route A (D2C):} The decisive signal is 8 to 10 qualified software engineers placing a paid, binding deposit for an intensive 8-week AI systems clinic, showing verifiable code mastery gains, and completing the course without dropping out.
  \item \textbf{For Route B (College):} The decisive signal is an authorized academic dean signing an official, priced purchase order for a spring semester lab curriculum with clear payment milestones and zero uncompensated faculty travel.
  \item \textbf{For Route C (Enterprise):} The decisive signal is an engineering director sponsoring a paid, bounded diagnostic audit with an explicit, approved budget line and agreed security permissions.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Prioritize the commercial experiment that produces verified behavioral commitment and repeatable economics with the least irreversible downside.}
