\section*{The Theory in 14 Principles}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The Theory in 14 Principles}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=16pt,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A market is a dynamic system of buyer decisions and commercial exchanges}, not a demographic headcount of people who could theoretically benefit.
  \item \textbf{Human need, verbal interest, willingness to pay, ability to pay, and verified commercial transactions} are 5 fundamentally distinct economic realities.
  \item \textbf{Define a market strictly at the intersection of a concrete buyer, functional job, purchasing trigger, available alternatives, and budget} before attempting to broaden it.
  \item \textbf{Functional substitutes, informal self-production, and non-consumption} constrain your pricing power and adoption far more brutally than direct competitors.
  \item \textbf{Rapid macroeconomic industry growth does not imply supplier profitability} or an accessible, defensible entry corridor for a newcomer.
  \item \textbf{Industrial market structure governs the distribution of economic surplus} among customers, producers, suppliers, platforms, and complementors.
  \item \textbf{Map where net profit and bargaining leverage actually accumulate across the value chain}, rather than chasing the layer with the largest headline revenue.
  \item \textbf{Treat TAM as an unproven macroeconomic hypothesis}, and build your operational plan strictly upon a bottom-up SOM bounded by customer acquisition and delivery capacity.
  \item \textbf{A genuine market gap is an economically painful, unserved job whose unit economics can support a specialist}, not an arbitrary empty quadrant on a competitor chart.
  \item \textbf{Entry barriers, exit liabilities, and the incumbent's rational economic response} dictate whether a commercial opening persists long enough to build an enterprise.
  \item \textbf{Competitive timing is the simultaneous alignment of 5 independent clocks}: technology, customer readiness, infrastructure, regulation, and founder runway.
  \item \textbf{A founder's specific capability to reach, persuade, and service a market} matters infinitely more than the sector's abstract theoretical attractiveness.
  \item \textbf{Execute bounded, low-cost behavioral experiments that force a decision} before committing scarce personal capital to large, irreversible business architectures.
  \item \textbf{The optimal initial market enables the founder to create authentic value, capture sufficient cash to survive, learn with maximum velocity, and accumulate the capabilities required to build an enduring institution.}
\end{enumerate}
