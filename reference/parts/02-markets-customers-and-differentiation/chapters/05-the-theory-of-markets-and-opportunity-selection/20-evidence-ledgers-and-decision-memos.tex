\section{Avoiding the Pseudo-Scientific Trap of Weighted Scoring}

Weighted multi-criteria decision matrices are widely used in corporate management consulting, but they frequently lead early-stage entrepreneurs into self-delusion.

Consider an opportunity that scores 5/5 on Market Size, 5/5 on Macro Trend, 1/5 on Customer Reachability, and 1/5 on Founder Capability. An uncritical arithmetic average yields 3.0/5.0, disguising the fatal reality that \textbf{the venture cannot acquire or service a single customer.}

Deploy a \textbf{non-compensatory evaluation discipline}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=12pt,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
  \item If an opportunity lacks customer reachability, affordable downside, or legal compliance, eliminate it immediately, regardless of its hypothetical market size.
  \item Compare remaining candidates using \textbf{dominance logic}: Is Option A superior or equal to Option B across all critical dimensions?
  \item When candidates are closely matched, select the opportunity that offers the \textbf{highest information velocity per rupee of capital and hour of founder time risked.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Pre-Committed Stop Conditions}

Before initiating an exploratory test, establish explicit, quantifiable \textbf{stop conditions} that will force an immediate pause or pivot:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=12pt,itemsep=2pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item More than 70\% of interested prospects reveal that they cannot or will not pay personal funds.
  \item Enterprise procurement demands security certifications or indemnity terms that the startup cannot legally provide.
  \item Customer acquisition cost in outbound channels exceeds 50\% of net tuition revenue.
  \item Delivering the promised educational transformation requires more than 4 hours of direct founder labor per student per week.
  \item More than 40\% of enrolled pilot learners fail to complete weekly assignments due to workplace fatigue.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{A failed experiment is a brilliant investment in strategic knowledge only if you possess the courage to recognize failure.} Otherwise, customer research degenerates into an elaborate psychological defense mechanism to justify pre-existing biases.

\section{The 2-Page Executive Market-Selection Memo}

Conclude your market investigation by drafting a concise 2-page decision memorandum:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=14pt,itemsep=2pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{The Specific Buyer and Job:} Who is the exact decision-maker, what operational failure triggers their action, and what progress are they buying?
  \item \textbf{The Next-Best Alternative:} How do they solve or endure this problem today, and why does their workaround leave a costly gap?
  \item \textbf{Empirical Demand Evidence:} What verified real-world behaviors (not polite verbal praise) prove that this is a top-3 operational priority?
  \item \textbf{Market Boundary and Accessible Size:} How many reachable buyer units exist, and what is the realistic bottom-up first-year SOM?
  \item \textbf{Value Chain and Profit Retention:} Where do industry profits settle, who controls the primary bottleneck, and what preserves our margin?
  \item \textbf{Customer Acquisition Channel:} Through what exact, verified outbound or community channel will the first 10 customers be acquired?
  \item \textbf{Unit Economics and Delivery Capacity:} What is the realized price, direct variable cost, cash collection cycle, and founder hours required?
  \item \textbf{Founder Capability Fit:} Why does this founding team possess a distinct unfair advantage in delivering this specific solution?
  \item \textbf{Competitive Timing and Incumbent Response:} Why does this opening exist now, and how will incumbents react when we launch?
  \item \textbf{Maximum Downside and Pre-Committed Stop Rules:} What is the maximum affordable loss risked, and what specific empirical metric will trigger an exit?
\end{enumerate}

If you cannot complete these 10 sections with concrete facts, you have not understood the market deeply enough to justify writing code.
