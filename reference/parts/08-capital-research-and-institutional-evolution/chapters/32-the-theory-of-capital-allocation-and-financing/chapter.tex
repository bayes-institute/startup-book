\ChapterOpener{32}{The Theory of Capital Allocation and Financing}{Corporate Finance, Reinvestment, and Financial Forensics}{How founders understand money, price risk, preserve ownership, finance growth, and turn scarce capital into enduring institutional capability}

\begin{quote}
\itshape
\textbf{Central thesis.} Corporate finance for an entrepreneur is the discipline of deciding \textbf{which promises and capabilities deserve scarce money, whose capital should fund them, on what contractual terms, with what exposure to failure, and under whose control}. The purpose is not to maximize a spreadsheet's valuation or the amount raised. It is to fund projects that increase durable value while preserving the company's ability to fulfill obligations, adapt, and survive mistakes.
\end{quote}

\section*{Orientation and Chapter Scope}

Chapter 17 explained business models, unit economics and financial accounting. Chapter 18 explained liquidity and survival. Chapters 27-31 addressed scaling, moats, adjacency, partnerships and internationalization. This chapter integrates them into \textit{capital allocation}: choose between competing uses of funds; distinguish economic returns from accounting earnings; value uncertain investments; choose financing and ownership structures; read statements skeptically; govern consequential decisions. Chapter 33 will address research ventures specifically, Chapter 34 physical expansion, and Chapter 35 institutional governance. Their subjects appear here where financing logic requires them, but are developed further later.

This text covers the substance a private-company founder must know, not every instrument used by public companies, banks or multinational treasury departments. All numbers below are \textbf{hypothetical exercises}, not actual Bayes Institute revenue, fundraising proposals, valuation, debt capacity, or expected performance. Financial formulas depend on accounting definitions and comparable periods. Discount rates are assumptions, not objective constants. Examples using US SAFEs describe that contractual regime; \textbf{do not assume a US SAFE can be used unchanged by an Indian company}. Indian company law, FEMA/FDI, taxation and educational regulation can be transaction-specific. Obtain qualified accounting, tax and legal advice before executing securities, loans, grants, related-party transactions or foreign investment.
