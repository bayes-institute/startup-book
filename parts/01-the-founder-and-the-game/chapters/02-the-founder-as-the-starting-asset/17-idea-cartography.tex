\section{Idea Cartography: Drawing the Territory}

\subsection{The 3-Axis Search Space}

This section does not replace the rigorous market selection in Chapter 5. Its purpose is to construct a \textbf{founder-informed search space}: a set of directions worth investigating based on who you are and what you can touch.

I suggest a 3-axis coordinate map:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=11pt,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=1.5pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Axis 1: Capabilities and insights you can deploy.} Programming, statistical reasoning, curriculum architecture, manufacturing operations, procurement diagnosis, research design.
  \item \textbf{Axis 2: Customer groups you can understand or reach.} Working developers, engineering colleges, finance teams, plant supervisors, independent professionals.
  \item \textbf{Axis 3: Consequential problems or desired shifts.} Unplanned downtime, poor learner retention, expensive reconciliation, slow compliance, security vulnerabilities.
\end{itemize}

An opportunity hypothesis emerges where 1 item from each axis collides:

\begin{center}
\textbf{Capability $\times$ Reachable Customer $\times$ Costly Problem $\rightarrow$ Candidate Offer.}
\end{center}

\subsection{The Opportunity Card}

For every promising candidate, write 1 card strictly bounded to 1 page:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=11pt,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=1.5pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Customer:} Specific persona and purchasing authority.
  \item \textbf{Problem:} The exact unwanted friction or loss experienced.
  \item \textbf{Current alternative:} What they do today (including doing nothing).
  \item \textbf{Consequence:} Cost in wasted cash, delays, or missed revenue.
  \item \textbf{Founder advantage:} Why your specific background reduces risk.
  \item \textbf{Candidate 1st offer:} The smallest honest bounded result you can sell.
  \item \textbf{Access route:} How you will secure 5 discovery conversations.
  \item \textbf{Unit economics:} Estimated founder hours and delivery expenses.
  \item \textbf{Critical unknown:} The single fact that could invalidate the idea.
  \item \textbf{Affordable test:} What you will execute within 14 days to test it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The opportunity card forces a founder to name an actual customer, an economic consequence, and an affordable falsification experiment, preventing vague slogans from masquerading as strategy.}

\subsection{The 3 Opportunity Shelves}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=11pt,itemsep=1.5pt,topsep=1.5pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Act now:} Fits current capabilities and constraints; has a testable route to paying demand.
  \item \textbf{Investigate later:} Promising, but missing 1 or 2 critical capabilities or access channels.
  \item \textbf{Not for us now:} Requires capital, regulatory clearance, or scale far beyond early-stage bounds.
\end{itemize}
