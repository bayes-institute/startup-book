\section*{Orientation and Scope}

It is possible to sell more and become weaker. It is possible to hire more and reduce output. It is possible to increase revenue while decreasing the economic value produced per scarce hour, and possible to automate work while making the customer outcome less reliable. Growth is an observation about size. Scaling is a claim about the structure of an enterprise: that a valuable activity can expand through a repeatable system whose essential constraints are understood and responsibly extended.

\textbf{Scope and evidence note.} This chapter joins capacity economics, queueing theory, resource-based theories of firm growth, quality management, cash-flow discipline, organizational development, and experimental strategy. Classical frameworks are used as analytical lenses, not universal laws or predictive guarantees. Every Bayes Institute cohort size, price, cost, capacity ratio, performance figure, and growth scenario in this chapter is explicitly \textbf{hypothetical}, not historical evidence of Bayes revenue, enrollment, hiring, accreditation, or outcomes. The institute remains at an exploratory founding stage. All thresholds given as examples must be recalibrated using actual buyer behavior, delivery measurements, cash commitments, and applicable regulations.
