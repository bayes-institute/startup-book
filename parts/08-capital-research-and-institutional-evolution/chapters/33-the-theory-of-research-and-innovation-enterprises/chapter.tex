\ChapterOpener{33}{The Theory of Research and Innovation Enterprises}{Scientific Laboratories, Tech Transfer, and IP Portfolios}{How founders decide what to investigate, finance uncertainty, build laboratories, govern scientific knowledge, and turn discoveries into useful institutions and ventures}

\begin{quote}
\itshape
\textbf{Central thesis.} Research is a disciplined purchase of uncertain knowledge. An effective research enterprise must make 2 different promises: that the questions merit investigation and that the process of investigation is trustworthy: even when the answer is disappointing. Its founder must decide what uncertainty to buy, at what cost, with which talent and facilities, under what ownership and publication rules, and when to discontinue or translate the work.
\end{quote}

\section*{Orientation and Chapter Scope}

Chapter 32 established the fundamentals of corporate finance, capital allocation, and governance for growing enterprises. This chapter extends those disciplines into \textit{scientific and technological inquiry}: how to formulate falsifiable research questions, stage uncertainty, structure laboratories, manage reproducibility, fund non-linear discovery, govern background and foreground intellectual property, and translate laboratory findings into durable ventures and institutions.

\textbf{Evidence convention.} Throughout this chapter, [Evidence] denotes an attributed empirical finding or institutional rule; [Model] an illustrative mathematical calculation; [Hypothesis] a proposition a founder should empirically test; [Decision rule] a proposed policy rather than an immutable scientific law. Monetary examples are hypothetical INR amounts, excluding taxes unless explicitly stated. References [R1] to [R13] provide formal regulatory and academic foundations at the conclusion of this text.
